# Statistical Modeling of Sustainable Food Security and Agriculture in Ghana

## Project Overview
This project focuses on the statistical modeling and analysis of sustainable food security and agricultural self-sufficiency within the Ghanaian economy. The study evaluates Ghana’s agricultural production, food affordability, import dependency, and food self-sufficiency ratios using statistical modeling techniques and national agricultural datasets.

The research contributes to Sustainable Development Goal 2 (Zero Hunger) by providing data-driven insights into food security and sustainable agriculture in Ghana.

---

## Research Objectives
- Analyze Ghana’s food self-sufficiency across major crops
- Evaluate agricultural production and import dependency
- Develop statistical models for sustainable food security forecasting
- Assess Ghana’s progress toward Sustainable Development Goal 2 (SDG 2)
- Forecast future food self-sufficiency ratios for 2030
- Support policy decision-making through data analysis

## Statistical Models Implemented

### Self-Sufficiency Ratio (SSR)
Measures the percentage of domestic food consumption met through local production.

### Food Self-Sufficiency Ratio (FSSR)
Evaluates self-sufficiency for specific food crops such as:
- Rice
- Maize
- Tomatoes
- Cocoa
- Palm Oil
- Millet
- Yam
- Coffee

### Forecasting Models
The study projected food self-sufficiency trends for:
- Agricultural Production
- Imports
- Exports
- Food Consumption
- Hunger Index Trends

---

## Key Findings

### Major Insights
- Ghana achieved high self-sufficiency in cocoa, yam, and millet.
- Rice and tomatoes continue to rely heavily on imports.
- Cocoa remains a major export commodity for Ghana.
- Ghana’s food security indicators are projected to improve by 2030.
- Statistical projections suggest moderate improvement in hunger reduction.

### 2030 Forecast Highlights
Projected food self-sufficiency ratios indicate:
- Continued strength in cocoa production
- Near self-sufficiency in maize and millet
- Persistent import dependency for rice and tomatoes
